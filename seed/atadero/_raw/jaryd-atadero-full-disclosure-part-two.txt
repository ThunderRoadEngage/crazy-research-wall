                                  Part Two: The Evidence

        On June 4th, 2003, articles of clothing and human remains were found within ten days of each
other. Two businessmen from Fort Collins by the name of Robert Osborne and Gareth Watts deviated
from the Big South Trail, ascending the Crown Point mountainside only to allegedly discover clothing
identical to Jaryd’s the day he went missing. At a press conference, one piece of evidence had been
tampered with as noted by Jaryd’s grandfather, while a renowned medical examiner questioned why
they didn’t find even minuscule traces of human remains. Ten days after the initial discovery, LCSO
took a forensic recovery team up the mountain, finding the top of a skull and a tooth with broken roots.
During the initial search in 1999, a tracker had identified this area as a strong possibility of Jaryd’s
whereabouts due to the sign he found on the mountainside. The articles of evidence were found withing
35 feet of the area he pinpointed.
                                             The Tooth




Figure 1: Molar with broken roots. Photo courtesy LCSO.

The tooth was found resting on the remains of a rotting log, clean and undisturbed by nature. In
addition to being both clean and uncovered by debris, two of the roots were broken off. Broken roots
on a tooth always indicates forced removal. On the now-defunct Facebook page based on Allyn
Atadero’s book, an individual claiming to be a dental assistant had her boss look at photos of the tooth.
He stated that the tooth looked to be from a subject between the ages of 5 and 8, the roots appearing to
be in resorption. The age range is correct, as Jaryd would have been 6 years old. How could the tooth
have been from a child aged 5 to 8? Why were no other bone fragments recovered?

DNA Analysis: Allyn had previously been allowed to handle the tooth. Tests were performed by the
Colorado Bureau of Investigation. Results were given to Allyn without explanation. In an email sent to
Allyn Atadero, a third party forensic expert noted that the electropherogram readout indicated two sets
of DNA present. This would not have occurred had the tooth been properly cleaned. He also noted
distinct terminology errors in the DNA report that would not be permitted by experienced lab analysts,
using the terms locus (singular) and loci (plural) interchangeably. Analysts are typically hypersensitive
about using the correct terminology.
                                           The Skull Cap




Figure 2: Scratches and skulls. (Photo: LCSO)


The skull was found under a tree, upside down and partially filled with forest debris several feet from
the tooth.

DNA Anomalies: Tests conducted by CBI gleaned an 87% DNA match due to being in the elements for
four years, and could not issue a positive match. A firm in Texas received the remains for a second test.
After being hit by Hurricane Katrina, the firm was inundated with remains of victims. They became
frustrated with his calling, and told him he would not find the results he was looking for. After more
persistent badgering, they told Allyn what he wanted to hear and the remains were returned to him. A
third firm, Technical Associates in California, tested the skull and confirmed a match 16 years after
Jaryd when missing.

The marks on the skull, as noted previously in Part One, are not consistent with feeding animals. An
adult mountain lion does not feed on the skull of its prey, and if it had in this instance the skull cap
would have been in pieces.
                                            The Jacket




Figure 3: After four years exposed to the elements.


The jacket showed no signs of an attack. Rob Osborne did his best to demonstrate to Allyn that a
series of holes near the right shoulder were from the claws of a mountain lion pouncing on Jaryd.
Since mountain lions go for the entrails first, it would make sense that the jacket would be shredded
in the abdominal area and covered in blood. No blood or other fluid stains were present. No hairs
analyzed by CBI were from a mountain lion or any other predatory animal and remain undisclosed.

The jacket was made out of artificial fleece fibers, which do not hold up well to the elements. After
four winters in the mountains, being soaked with rain, snow, sleet, and exposed to the sun and wind, it
is hard to imagine that a jacket of this material would have lasted more than a year.

                                                 Shoes

Nearly new on the outside with very little forest debris inside, as well as lacking any trace of human
remains. It was stated that Jaryd hated his shoes being tied, and insisted on undoing the laces. This is an
odd occurrence given the loose fitting shoes. A mountain lion hauling prey up the side of a thickly
wooded mountain leaves no chance of both shoes remaining on the feet.

Allyn did an ad-hoc test where he left an identical pair of shoes outside to observe the effects of nature.
In less than two years the shoes quickly showed signs of yellowing than those found on the mountain.
