        On October 2nd, 1999, 3-year-old Jaryd Atadero went missing while on a group hike along the
Big South Trail, located 43 miles northwest of Fort Collins. It remains unsolved. If you are unfamiliar
with this case, please click here to read a detailed summary of the case.

                                       Section One: Theories

       Discussing any missing person’s case with the public comes with the inevitable cascades of
theories and speculations. While there is nothing inherently wrong with exchanging ideas regarding
different possibilities (my network and I have privately done our fair share), it will always prove to be a
double-edged sword when trying to whittle down to the truth. Here we will deal with the most
prevalent theories regarding Jaryd’s disappearance so that we can have a proper context going forward.
This should not discourage people coming forward with their thoughts, but rather encourage interested
parties to do so on a factual foundation.


1. The Mountain Lion Theory – Despite being the most prevalent and argued non-abduction theory of
Jaryd’s disappearance, the much-debated mountain lion attack scenario has the most evidence against
it. During the initial search week, trackers had come across mountain lion prints near the area Jaryd
was last seen. These tracks intersected with what one experienced tracker thought was the track of a
small child. There was a dispute between the trackers as to whether or not the tracks were actually
made by a child or were that of a bear, which can sometimes resemble a human footprint.


Yet regardless of the type of tracks, three individuals from the Division of Wildlife concluded that the
mountain lion tracks were older than the smaller prints, which were likely those of a bear. The tracker
who discovered them, “Seaux”, told Allyn he was certain the cat never saw Jaryd. DOW personnel
agreed, and could not find any evidence of an attack anywhere or any evidence to support the theory.
(Supplemental Field Incident Report 99-06-59.S02, Pg. 5.)

indicates that they were removed from, or by, the victim prior to the time that any significant bleeding

“Further, no evidence was recovered that would provide grounds for rejecting the view that predation

No blood, no decomposition fluids, not a single trace of DNA on the clothing. Hairs found on the
clothing were discovered to be non-human and non-mountain lion, which we will discuss later.


2. The Drowning Theory – Allyn Atadero pressed Sheriff Alderden during a phone call to come clean
about what he thought happened to Jaryd after he admitted that he pushed the mountain lion theory to
appease the press. He stated matter-of-factly that he thought Jaryd drowned in the river and wouldn’t be
recovered for years due to cold waters.


Yet the river was at its seasonal low, with large frequent patches of bare riverbed. The Sheriff’s report
indicates that dive teams who searched the Cache La Poudre River for Jaryd’s body reported a 95-
100% probability of detection (Supplemental Field Incident Report 99-06-59.S02, Pg. 6). Even when
the river began to get covered with snow and ice during the initial search week, divers reported 60%
probability of detection. Having worked a long stretch of river several times with multiple teams, it was
virtually impossible for even the body of a small child to go unnoticed.
While Allyn was running his Facebook page for Jaryd, a curious conversation took place between he
and an anthropologist, the latter of whom commented on fine scratches and marks on the skull. She
stated confidently that they were made by being washed over river rock. She didn’t realize at this time
how high up on the ridge they had discovered the remains. Once she realized this, the conversation was
subsequently deleted, but Allyn kept it saved. I’m not a forensic expert, but I compared the marks to
other known examples of skeletal remains recovered from bodies of water and they appear very
consistent. I mention this because I do not believe in coincidences, and the fact that drowning was the
Sheriff’s preferred theory in the case is quite odd. We will discuss this more in a later document.


4. The Injury Theory – Though rarely discussed, the theory that Jaryd had become injured somewhere
along the trail (and in at least one case, fell victim to a scavenging mountain lion after injury or death)
has been thoroughly debunked by the highly sensitive noses of tracking dogs. After the initial search
had been suspended, a handler took their dog in cadaver mode along Pingree Park Rd that runs through
the southern ridge of Poudre Canyon. The dog caught a scent, which led them to an old mine shaft with
a collapsed entrance. After authorities removed the debris, they recovered the body of a teenage murder
victim who had been abducted and killed seventeen years earlier. A scent would have been much more
easily recovered along the trail, especially after they obtained proper scent articles (Discussed in Part
Three: The Evidence, TBA.) An injured child would scream or cry for a prolonged period or, in the
event of unconsciousness or death, remained where he entered into rest. A scent impression would have
been much stronger had he remained on the trail.
5. The Bigfoot Theory – Popularized by a certain author whose name shall go unrecognized, “Jaryd’s”
clothing was found in disarray at a point on the mountain roughly 500 vertical feet above the trail. After
the clothing was recovered, waves were made through the press which included a telling interview on
Fox News with renowned medical examiner, Dr. Michael Baden. He specifically questioned the
mountain lion attack theory, citing the lack of small bones or bone fragments in the shoes, saying you’d
at least find a tooth. Ten days later, a recovery team was sent up the mountain to search for remains,
finding the skull cap. Inches away from that, a lone tooth sitting on a log, clean with broken roots.


I highly doubt Bigfoot was watching the news when Dr. Michael Baden spoke about the tooth, and
realized he’d made some kind mistake in his effort to make it look like a mountain lion had taken
Jaryd. Staging evidence just doesn’t seem like his style.
